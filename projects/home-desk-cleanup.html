Desk cleanup: the pen task.

Pick a pen off the desk and drop it in the bin. I tried several policies and observed how their behavior changed with the pen, its orientation, and the drop target.

Status Ongoing
Data 200 teleoperated episodes, 3 sessions
Policies ACT · SmolVLA · Diffusion · MolmoAct
Notable observation MolmoAct 2 picked up two pens in the run shown

Merged dataset

I collected two datasets, both teleoperated on the same table:

  • 40 episodes with a single pen dropped into a bin, varying the pen's location.
  • 60 episodes with several differently coloured pens on the table at varied positions, picking up one of them, and with the pouch shifted slightly between episodes.

I merged the two and trained SmolVLA and a diffusion policy on the combined set.

Neither policy completed the task reliably in the trials I ran. Object count, colour, position and pouch location all differ between the two datasets, which makes the resulting behavior difficult to interpret.

What I observed

These trials produced three behaviors worth noting.

  • ACT completed the task with the orange and purple pens in the conditions tried, but failed with the green, blue and brown pens. This is consistent with sensitivity to visual appearance, although these trials do not establish which image features the policy relied on.
  • When I removed or changed the pouch, ACT still released the pen near the original drop location. This is consistent with learning a location-specific action rather than responding to the visible container.
  • ACT also failed in the significantly rotated-pen condition I tried. More trials would be needed to characterize how sensitive it is to orientation.

In the pouch-removed trial, the policy continued executing without an obvious indication that the target was missing. From this behavior alone, I cannot determine what internal representation it learned.

Related ideas from the literature

The color sensitivity resembles what the literature describes as shortcut learning: a policy may rely on a feature that correlates with success in the demonstrations but is not central to the intended task. The fixed drop behavior is also reminiscent of causal confusion in imitation learning. These concepts offer possible interpretations of what I observed, rather than diagnoses established by the current experiments.

MolmoAct: swapping in a VLM backbone

MolmoAct 2, Ai2's action-reasoning model on the Molmo2-ER VLM backbone, used the same 40 demonstrations as ACT. In the conditions I tried, it completed the task with additional pen colors that ACT failed on. Because the models differ in more than just their visual backbones, these trials do not isolate the reason for the difference.

In the run shown below, it also picked up two pens one at a time and placed them in the bin.

Observations from the demonstrations

A possible grasp signal from gripper position

Every frame records what the gripper was told to do and where it actually got to. Told to shut but unable to, something may be in the way. In this dataset, that heuristic identified the apparent grasp interval in 99 of 100 episodes. The remaining episode was also the one in which the grasp visibly failed.

The demonstrations contained less variation at placement

In the 40 episodes ACT trained on, shoulder_pan (base rotation) spreads 10.2 units at pick and 2.3 at place: 4.5x tighter at the container than at the object. Every release lands between −8.5 and −1.3. The release configurations were more tightly clustered than the grasp configurations. This does not establish what ACT learned, but it offers a possible explanation for the location-specific release behavior observed later.
